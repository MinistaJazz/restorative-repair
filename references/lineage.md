# Lineage  -  full citations

This skill draws on a specific lineage of Black feminist abolitionist, disability justice, and restorative practice scholarship. Citations are in-body and load-bearing, not appendix. The names are the receipts.

Each source below names *what specifically this skill draws from*  -  not generic homage.

---

## Directly operationalized into the response architecture

## Mia Mingus
**"The Four Parts of Accountability."** *Leaving Evidence*, December 18, 2019.
https://leavingevidence.wordpress.com/2019/12/18/the-four-parts-of-accountability-how-to-give-a-genuine-apology-part-1/

> "Self-reflection. Apology. Repair. Behavior change."

**What this skill draws from Mingus:** The four-part structure of the response architecture. Mingus's framework is the foundational structure RRP builds on. Where Mingus writes for human accountability, this skill ports the structure to agent output repair. The four parts are not original to RRP  -  they are Mingus's. RRP's contribution is to embed them as a response-layer practice for AI agents.

**Pod mapping.** Bay Area Transformative Justice Collective.
https://batjc.wordpress.com/pods-and-pod-mapping-worksheet/

**What this skill draws:** The concept that accountability is held in a pod, not in a single relationship. Informs the routing component of crisis-handoff failure  -  the agent does not become the user's pod, the agent points back to the user's pod.

---

## Harriet Lerner
**Why Won't You Apologize? Healing Big Betrayals and Everyday Hurts.** Touchstone, 2017.

> "A heartfelt apology requires the offender to come from a place of empathy."

**What this skill draws from Lerner:** The specific-acknowledgment requirement in Part 1. Lerner is explicit that vague apologies ("I'm sorry if you were hurt") do harm by transferring the burden of definition to the injured party. Part 1 of RRP enforces specificity precisely to avoid this failure mode.

---

## Aaron Lazare
**On Apology.** Oxford University Press, 2004.

> "An apology, even though it sometimes only takes a few seconds, is one of the most profound interactions of which civilized people are capable."

**What this skill draws from Lazare:** Lazare's empirical taxonomy of effective apology  -  what makes an apology actually work  -  informs the response architecture's degrees of freedom. Lazare names four basic acts of effective apology that map closely to RRP's four parts. The structure is consistent across two decades of independent restorative scholarship; RRP does not invent it, RRP encodes it.

## Framing lineage

## Mariame Kaba
**We Do This 'Til We Free Us: Abolitionist Organizing and Transforming Justice.** Haymarket Books, 2021.

> "Hope is a discipline."

**What this skill draws from Kaba:** The abolitionist frame for accountability without punishment. The skill's refusal to make the agent into a villain (the over-apologetic spiral) is rooted in Kaba's insistence that accountability and punishment are distinct, and that conflating them perpetuates carceral logic inside relationships.

---

## Dominic Barter
**Restorative Circles.** Developed in Brazil, beginning in the 1990s, and shared internationally through restorative justice and Nonviolent Communication networks.
https://www.restorativecircles.org/

**Restorative Justice Project, Center for Nonviolent Communication.**
https://www.cnvc.org/

**What this skill draws from Barter:** The author trained in Restorative Circles under Dominic Barter. Barter's work supplies the circle-process lineage beneath this skill: conflict is not merely an individual error to be punished or privately corrected, but a community-held rupture that can be transformed through structured dialogue, shared meaning-making, and mutually beneficial action. This skill does not implement a full Restorative Circle. It translates one narrow piece of that lineage into an AI response layer: when the agent causes or reveals harm, the agent must name the rupture, include the affected user's reality, offer repair as action, and change future behavior with an observable marker.

**Relationship to NVC:** Restorative Circles are closely linked to Nonviolent Communication practice and to Barter's work coordinating restorative justice efforts through CNVC. In this skill, Barter is the bridge between NVC grammar and restorative justice process.

---

## Marshall B. Rosenberg
**Nonviolent Communication: A Language of Life.** PuddleDancer Press, 3rd edition, 2015.

**What this skill draws:** The four-part NVC structure (observation, feeling, need, request) is the composable NVC layer in the toggle stack. When NVC is toggled on alongside `restorative-repair`, the response architecture is shaped by NVC's grammar. The two compose cleanly because both refuse demand language in favor of invitation.

---

## Resmaa Menakem
**My Grandmother's Hands: Racialized Trauma and the Pathway to Mending Our Hearts and Bodies.** Central Recovery Press, 2017.

> "Trauma is not a flaw or a weakness. It is a highly effective tool of safety and survival."

**What this skill draws from Menakem:** The somatic frame and racialized-trauma awareness. The trauma-informed-pacing toggle in the composable stack is rooted in Menakem's insistence that repair without somatic awareness re-injures. When the somatic check-in layer is on, it is Menakem's work being honored.

---

## adrienne maree brown
**Emergent Strategy: Shaping Change, Changing Worlds.** AK Press, 2017.

> "Small is good, small is all. The large is a reflection of the small."

**What this skill draws from brown:** The composability frame  -  that practice layers can compose like fractals, that small consistent practices scale, that the design of any single intervention should mirror the desired design of the whole system. The toggle stack architecture is a brown-shaped move.

---

## Patty Berne et al. / Sins Invalid
**Skin, Tooth, and Bone: The Basis of Movement Is Our People.** Sins Invalid, 2019.

> "Access intimacy is that elusive, hard to describe feeling when someone else 'gets' your access needs."

**What this skill draws from Sins Invalid:** Disability justice / access intimacy as the relational frame for what a useful AI accommodation does. The skill's recognition test (does the skill teach by doing?) is rooted in disability justice's insistence that performance of accessibility is not accessibility  -  only the actual experience of access is access.

---

## The Combahee River Collective
**The Combahee River Collective Statement.** April 1977.
https://www.blackpast.org/african-american-history/combahee-river-collective-statement-1977/

> "If Black women were free, it would mean that everyone else would have to be free..."

**What this skill draws from Combahee:** A core frame for the Much Different World skill stack. Combahee establishes that liberation is most rigorously pursued when the most multiply-oppressed are centered, and that flattening voices to fit existing systems is a form of harm. RRP refuses the flatten  -  every Part of the response architecture is anti-flatten.

## On lineage as practice

This document is not an appendix. It is the spine of the skill.

The cited scholars' work was authored for human relationships and community accountability. The adaptation of that work into AI agent response architecture is the author's move, is not endorsed by the cited scholars, and the citation is an act of accountability to the lineage, not a claim of their approval.

If you build on this work, cite these sources too. The citation network is the structural copy-defense for any skill in the RSM family. If your derived work cites only "Minista Jazz" without the lineage, the work is incomplete. If your derived work cites only the lineage without Minista Jazz, the work is dishonest about how it reached you.

Both names. Both moves. That is the practice.
