### Why this exists

When an AI agent makes a mistake, common agent-apology patterns tend to fall into three buckets: customer-support apology scripts (image management), silent correction (the system changes course without naming what happened), and over-apologetic spirals (which flip the user into comforting the agent). This skill refuses all three.

The skill implements the four-part restorative response architecture (acknowledge specifically, name the deeper pattern, offer specific repair, change behavior with an observable marker) at the response surface, without retraining the underlying model, and inside a registrant-governed covenant when one is present.

### Connection to Anthropic's May 2026 alignment research

Anthropic's "Teaching Claude Why" (May 8, 2026) demonstrated that demonstration-plus-deliberation training is substantially more efficient than demonstration-only training for AI alignment. This skill borrows the same design lesson and applies it at the response layer: the four-part protocol requires a structured account of what happened, why it happened, what repair is offered, and what marker changes next.

Source: Anthropic, "Teaching Claude why" (May 8, 2026). https://www.anthropic.com/research/teaching-claude-why
